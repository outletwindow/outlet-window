# Outlet Window — Data Privacy and Protection

Document ID: OW-DATA-PRIVACY-001
Version: 1.0
Status: Draft — Pending Legal and Privacy Review

## 1. Purpose
Establish privacy and personal data protection requirements across Outlet Window products, services, operations, AI systems and third-party integrations.

## 2. Scope
Applies to:
- Customers.
- Merchants and store employees.
- Couriers and shoppers.
- Administrators and company personnel.
- Business partners and service providers.
- Personal data processed by AI systems.

## 3. Privacy Principles
- Collect only data necessary for a defined purpose.
- Explain why data is collected.
- Process data under a valid legal basis.
- Keep data accurate and appropriately updated.
- Limit access to authorized users.
- Retain data only as long as necessary or legally required.
- Protect data throughout its lifecycle.
- Respect applicable privacy rights.

## 4. Data Classification
Classify data into:
- Public.
- Internal.
- Confidential.
- Restricted personal data.

Sensitive and high-risk data requires stronger access controls and protection.

## 5. Data Categories
Potential categories include:
- Account and contact information.
- Delivery addresses.
- Location information.
- Order and transaction records.
- Merchant and workforce information.
- Customer support communications.
- Device and security logs.
- Identity verification information where legally required.

Payment card data should be handled by approved payment providers whenever possible. Outlet Window should avoid storing full card details.

## 6. Purpose and Legal Basis
Document the purpose and applicable legal basis for each processing activity.

Examples:
- Account creation and authentication.
- Order processing and delivery.
- Payment and settlement.
- Fraud prevention and security.
- Customer support.
- Legal and regulatory obligations.
- Optional marketing and personalization.

Do not assume consent is the legal basis for every activity.

## 7. Location Privacy
- Request device location permission when needed.
- Provide manual address selection.
- Explain location use.
- Limit courier GPS tracking to legitimate operational needs.
- Avoid continuous tracking outside active tasks.
- Restrict location history access.
- Apply retention limits to location records.

## 8. Data Sharing
Before sharing data with a third party:
- Identify the recipient.
- Define the purpose.
- Confirm legal authority and contractual safeguards.
- Share the minimum necessary data.
- Document cross-border transfers where applicable.
- Review subprocessors and access permissions.

## 9. AI Data Protection
- Do not send unnecessary personal data to AI providers.
- Use masking or pseudonymization where practical.
- Review provider retention and training policies.
- Restrict agent access by role and task.
- Prevent AI agents from disclosing private customer information.
- Log sensitive AI access.
- Require human review for high-impact decisions.

AI recommendations must not automatically determine legal rights, refunds, worker penalties or other high-impact outcomes without appropriate review.

## 10. Data Subject Rights
Provide processes to handle applicable requests, including:
- Access to personal data.
- Correction of inaccurate information.
- Deletion where legally applicable.
- Restriction or objection where applicable.
- Data portability where applicable.
- Withdrawal of consent where consent is the legal basis.

Requests must be authenticated and tracked.

## 11. Retention and Deletion
Create a retention schedule for each data category.

- Retain operational data only as needed.
- Preserve financial and legal records for required periods.
- Delete or anonymize data when retention is no longer justified.
- Apply deletion to backups according to documented backup lifecycle rules.
- Maintain evidence of approved deletion operations.

## 12. Security Controls
- Encryption in transit and at rest.
- Least-privilege access.
- Multi-factor authentication for privileged accounts.
- Audit logging.
- Secure backup and recovery.
- Vulnerability management.
- Incident response procedures.
- Regular access reviews.

## 13. Privacy by Design
Privacy requirements must be considered during:
- Product design.
- Database architecture.
- API development.
- AI agent design.
- Merchant integrations.
- Analytics and reporting.
- New country launches.

High-risk processing must receive a privacy impact assessment before launch.

## 14. Data Breach Management
When a suspected breach occurs:
1. Report and record the incident.
2. Contain unauthorized access.
3. Preserve evidence.
4. Assess affected data and individuals.
5. Determine legal notification requirements and deadlines.
6. Notify relevant authorities and affected individuals when required.
7. Document remediation and lessons learned.

## 15. Saudi and International Compliance
Before launch in each market, identify applicable privacy and data protection laws.

For Saudi Arabia, assess applicable Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), its regulations and relevant guidance from the competent authority.

For international markets, assess local privacy, transfer, consumer and sector-specific requirements.

Do not assume one country's compliance automatically satisfies another country's laws.

## 16. Privacy Governance
Assign:
- Data owners.
- Privacy lead or responsible officer.
- Security team.
- System owners.
- Vendor privacy responsibilities.

Maintain a record of processing activities and privacy decisions.

## 17. Acceptance Criteria
- Data inventory and classification exist.
- Each processing purpose is documented.
- Legal basis is identified.
- Privacy notices are prepared.
- Location permissions are limited and transparent.
- Third-party sharing is controlled.
- Retention and deletion schedules are defined.
- Data subject request workflows are documented.
- Privacy impact assessments are completed for high-risk processing.
- Breach response procedures are tested.

## 18. Implementation Status
This document defines requirements and does not claim that Outlet Window has completed legal registration, privacy assessments, regulatory approvals or operational privacy controls.

Final implementation requires qualified legal review in each launch market.
